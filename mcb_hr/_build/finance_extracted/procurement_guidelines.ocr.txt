Section 01 — Overview

Considering human as the supreme being and more valuable than all the wealth of the
world Mukti Cox’s Bazar has stared its journey at the Cox’s Bazar districts On 1996 as a
non-government development organization to materialize the dream a peaceful
universal society. From the very beginning of the organization, it has been implementing
various programs especially for the poor and vulnerable women making them aware of
various livelihood issues through family approach at urban and rural areas of Cox’s Bazar
district.

To materialize this dream, its effort is going on since 1996. With the help of all concerned
its name and fame is spreading all over the country. We have firm belief that in near
future MCB will become familiar to the international arena. The workers and all
concerned adhere to the core belief that MCB is the organization of those for whom we
are working, a bit advance of the poor, helpless, oppressed, and unprivileged women.
Steps were and are being taken to save them from the clutches of poverty and social
instrument of tyranny. But if we are let to speak the truth, we are not ready to call these
measures adequate or very effective to bring overnight changes in a society where
problems are accumulated for ages. Help with money should not be a system or
procedure of uplift. Money may be given to promote business, to create job and income
generating activities. But to keep these necessary creation and prevailing machineries of
income sustainable and functioning ethical, logistic, and technical supports are being
given which we think will bring socio-economic changes in the target society. Alongside
we are trying to link with the administration and peacekeeping agencies to guarantee
and preserve their basic rights. Through different projects designed for the purpose we
have taken substantial measures to make them aware of their short comings,
impediments, and problems they are facing, the prejudices and superstitions they are
becoming target of, and to find out way to get rid of all these. We have ambition to make
our target society a poverty-free welfare society where men, women and children can
live in peace and harmony, with minimum dignity and honor, with liberty and freedom.

The policies described in this section apply to all purchases made by MCB. MCB requires
the practice of ethical, responsible, and reasonable procedures related to purchasing,
agreements and contracts, and related forms of commitment. The policies in this section
describe the principles and procedures that all staff shall adhere to in the completion of
their designated responsibilities.

The goal of these procurement policies is to ensure that materials and services are
obtained in an effective manner and in compliance with the provisions of applicable
statutes, policies, procedures, and executive orders.

1.2 Title and Commencement
These policies shall be known as the “Mukti Cox’s Bazar Procurement Guidelines
2018” and the revised policy shall come into force with effect from October 01, 2023.

1.3 Objectives

The objectives for establishing and applying these procurement guidelines are:
i). To bring efficiency and effectiveness with a view to contributing towards
achievement of MCB objectives;
ii). To ensure transparency, fairness, and integrity in procurement process;
iii). To ensure competitiveness in the procurement process through adherence
to clear guidelines so that best value for money may be attained.
Goods or services supplied should be of the required quantity and quality,
available within the time required and at the lowest acceptable cost in order to
deliver the best value of money.
This means selecting offers which present the optimum combination of factors
such as appropriate quality, life-cycle cost, delivery time, and other parameters
which can include social, environmental, and other strategic objectives which
meet end-user needs.
Best value does not necessarily mean the lowest price option, but rather,
represents the best return on investment, taking into consideration the
evaluation criteria in the specified solicitation documents;
iv). To devise a balanced system of procedures that can be applied during normal
as well as emergency situations, e.g., flood, earthquake, cyclone, and other
natural calamities.
v). To involve all stakeholders in the procurement process in a meaningful way so
that planning and coordination can be done timely and the right product or
services can be procured in the right quality and quantity and supplied to the
right place at the right time.
vi). To meet the requirement of donor agencies and other local and international
bodies, who are partners in progress with MCB, through reforming procurement
practices in line with modern donor agencies and commercial usage.

1.4 Applicability

These procurement guidelines shall be applicable for all procurement carried out by
MCB personnel irrespective of the sources of funds, local or foreign, own funds or
grants. In case any provision(s) stated in these procedures is/are not compatible with
the provisions required by any donor agency for implementation of projects or
programs funded by them, then those shall be highlighted, and waivers shall be
sought and special provisions shall be approved by the Chief Executive of the MCB.
However, those special provisions shall generally conform to the objectives of MCB
and should in no way hamper the proper and timely delivery of MCB programs and
services.

1.5 Overall Procurement Direction
MCB will follow a mainly centralized procurement strategy with a provision for
procurement at area/field level to meet the day-to-day requirements, emergencies
and purchase of food, local agricultural input/ products like as seedlings, vermi
composites, etc. and perishable items from vendors or direct beneficiaries of the
organization. A centralized procurement approach will be more focused on co-
ordination rather than centralization. Considering effectiveness and efficiency, this
approach is more suited for the organization. The reasons for a centralized approach
are economies of scale through aggregation of requirements, optimum use of
expertise, efficient storage, and distribution system as well as lower administration
costs. The MCB Procurement Team at Head Office is expected to take overall
responsibility of implementing procurement related activities effectively and

efficiently.

Section 02 — Responsibility for Purchasing

All department heads or their designees shall have the authority to initiate purchases on
behalf of their department, within the guidelines described here. Department in charge
shall inform the Procurement Department of all individuals that may initiate purchases
or prepare purchase orders. The Procurements Department shall maintain a current list
of all authorized purchasers.

The Procurement Department shall be responsible for all processing up to purchase
orders. The Head of Finance or Chairperson of Procurement Committee has approval
authority over all purchases, and shall make the final determination on any proposed
purchases where budgetary or other conditions may result in denial.

Procurement responsibilities for the various units of MCB and their personnel shall be as
under:

2.1 Procurement Department: A Procurement Department, suitably staffed, shall be
formed at the Head Office of the organization and under the Head of Accounts &
Finance. This team shall be responsible for processing of all procurements at the
central level. These include, but are not limited to, review of procurement plans and
cost estimates; preparing draft bidding documents; advertising; receiving of tenders,
proposals, and quotations; preparation of evaluation reports and recommendations
for award of contracts; preparation of draft contracts; and monitoring of
procurement and contracts. In addition to processing of procurement, this Team
shall serve as a facilitating Team and play a procurement advisory role to the other
Units of MCB.

2.2 Procurement Committee: The Chief Executive of MCB shall establish a standing
Procurement Committee (PC). The Procurement Committee (PC) will form with the
05 (five) members. The Head of Accounts & Finance shall chair the PC, The
Procurement Officer/Manager (Who will be in-charge of purchase department) shall
be the Secretary of the Committee, the concerned Project Head/Department Head
(minimum Sth grade as per organization's HR policy) will be included as a member of
the Procurement Committee as ex officio. The Chief Executive will elect two others.
The Chairperson of PC can appoint experts to provide advice on any complicated
and/or specialized procurement through consultation with the chief executive of the
organization. This expert will not be a member of the PC. On conclusion of evaluation
for each contract, the procurement team shall prepare the initial draft of an
Evaluation Report reflecting the views of PC, which shall include a recommendation
for award of contract, and submit comparative statements and reports to the
approving authority.

construction work, repair work, purchase of materials and for procurement of food

Section 03 — Code of Conduct in Purchasing

Ethical conduct in managing the MCB's purchasing activities is essential. Staff must
always be mindful that they represent the Executive Council and share a professional
trust with other staff and the general member.
¢ Staff shall discourage the offer of, and decline, individual gifts, or gratuities of value
in any way that might influence the purchase of supplies, equipment, and/or services.
e Staff shall notify their immediate supervisor if they are offered such gifts.
¢ No officer, EC member, employee, or agent shall participate in the selection or
administration of a vendor if a real or apparent conflict of interest would be involved.
Such a conflict would arise if an officer, board member, employee or agent, or any
member of his or her immediate family, his or her spouse or partner, or an organization
that employs or is about to employ any of the parties indicated herein, has a financial or
other interest in the vendor selected.
* Officers, board members, employees, and agents shall neither solicit nor accept
gratuities, favors, or anything of monetary value from vendors or parties to sub-
agreements.

3.1 Introduction: MCB is a nonprofit, organization. From time-to-time staffs’ members
and or governing body members may encounter conflicts of interest when
participating in MCB decision-making processes, as their professional or personal
roles intersect. Potential areas where a conflict of interest may arise include:
¢ Recruitment of staffs
e Procurement of goods and services
¢ Undertaking new projects or entering into new partnerships
e Representing MCB in other forums
Consequently, there exists between the MCB and its executive council, officers, and
management employees and the public a fiduciary duty, which carries with it an
Executive Council and unbending duty of loyalty and commitment. The EC, officers,
and management employees have the responsibility of administering the affairs of
the MCB honestly and prudently, and of exercising their best care, skill, and judgment
for the sole benefit of the MCB. Those persons shall exercise the utmost good faith
in all transactions involved in their duties, and they shall not use their positions within
the MCB or knowledge gained therein for their personal benefit. The interests of the
organization must be the priority in all decisions and actions.

conflict of interest, or in which the process of making a decision may create an
appearance of a conflict of interest. All board members and employees have an

5.3 Selection of consultants:

5.3.1 Selection of Consultancy Firms — Quality and Cost Base Selection (QCBS):
When Consulting firms are selected through a competitive process, Technical and
Financial QCBS shall be applied for any contracts estimated to cost on the ceiling
specified in Section - 07 Procurement Threshold. Proposals will receive separately
using a Request for Proposals (RFP) from minimum three consulting firms, who
are qualified and have performed similar assignments within the celling specified
in Section - 07 Procurement Threshold. In addition to price (Financial Proposal),
the quality of the Technical Proposals of the consultants shall be considered.
Depending on the complexity and importance of the assignment, the weight of
the Technical Proposals may range within 75 points, out of 100 points, with
higher weight being given to more complex and important assignments.
Financial Proposals shall be provided the residual points. The Technical Proposals
shall be evaluated first and shall be based on: (a) consultant’s specific experience,
(b) methodology proposed, and (c) key personnel proposed for the assignment.
More weights shall be given to qualifications of key personnel compared to
experience of the firm or the methodology. The RFP shall indicate the evaluation
criteria and the technical qualification score. On completion of the technical
evaluation, a Technical Evaluation Report shall be prepared and approved by the
Procurement Committee. The Financial Proposals of only the technically
qualified firms shall be opened and the combined (technical and financial)
evaluation shall be carried out. Contract shall be awarded to the firm that obtains
the highest combined technical and financial score.

5.3.2 Selection of Consultancy Firms — Fixed Budget Selection (FBS): The process
of FBS for contracts estimated to cost on the ceiling specified in in Section 07
Procurement Threshold is the same as Quality & Cost Based Selection (QCBS),
except that weight is not given to technical and financial proposals and the
budget is fixed and specified in the RFP. Technical proposals are evaluated first
and any firm that does not meet the minimum qualifying score is rejected.
Financial Proposals of only the firms that meet the minimum qualifying score are
opened. Any firm submitting Financial Proposal adjusted for cost of inputs stated
in their Technical Proposal but not included in the Financial Proposal, and higher
than the budget, is rejected. Contract is awarded to the firm that received the
highest technical score among the rest of the qualified firms.

Selection does not provide the benefits of competition, both for quality and cost.
Therefore, this method shall be used only on an exceptional basis. SSS may be
appropriate for: (a) tasks that represent a natural continuation of previous
assignment carried out by the consultant, (b) in emergency cases, (c) when only
one firm is qualified for the assignment.

9.1. Competition is normally the most effective method to ensure best value for money
is achieved. Although competitive bidding should be the general rule, exception may
apply in the following cases, subject to the provision of the necessary justification:

a. When there is no competitive marketplace for the requirement, such as where
a monopoly exists, where prices are fixed by legislation or government
regulation, or where the requirement involves a proprietary product or services;
b. When there has been a previous determination or there is a need to
standardize the requirement;

c. When offers for identical products and services have been obtained
competitively within a reasonable period (maximum three month) and condition
of contract and price remain competitive;

d. When, within a reasonable prior period, a formal solicitation has not produced
satisfactory results;

e. When there is an exigency for the requirement;

f. When the proposed procurement contract is for the purchase or lease of real
property and the market conditions do not allow effective competition;

g. When the proposed procurement contract relates to obtaining services that
cannot be evaluated objectively; and

h. When a formal solicitation will not give satisfactory results.

Where one or more of the above circumstances occur, the procurement committee may
decide to waive the requirement or the competitive bidding process with discussed the
chief executive of the organization. Such a decision shall be fully documented and
justified in writing and kept in the procurement file for later inspection.

Section 11 — Special Purchasing Conditions

Competition is normally the most effective method to ensure best value for money is
achieved. Although competitive bidding should be the general rule, exception may apply
in the following cases, subject to the provision of the necessary justification:
a. When there is no competitive marketplace for the requirement, such as where
a monopoly exists, where prices are fixed by legislation or government
regulation, or where the requirement involves a proprietary product or services;
b. When there has been a previous determination or there is a need to
standardize the requirement;
c. When offers for identical products and services have been obtained
competitively within a reasonable period (maximum three month) and condition
of contract and price remain competitive;
d. When, within a reasonable prior period, a formal solicitation has not produced
satisfactory results;
e. When there is an exigency for the requirement;
f. When the proposed procurement contract is for the purchase or lease of real
property and the market conditions do not allow effective competition;
g. When the proposed procurement contract relates to obtaining services that
cannot be evaluated objectively; and
h. When a formal solicitation will not give satisfactory results.
Emergencies:
Where equipment, materials, parts, and/or services are needed, quotations may not be
necessary if the health, welfare, safety, etc., of staff and protection of Organization
property is involved.
Single Distributor/Source:
Sole source purchases may be made only when solicitation of multiple vendors is not
feasible and one of the following conditions applies:
a) The item or service is only available from one source;
b) The situation is a public emergency (Natural disaster, Man made disaster, etc.);
c) The funding/donor agency approves the purchase;
d) Competition is deemed inadequate (insufficient bidders).
A cost/price analysis is required and approval from the funding agency may be necessary
if the purchase is over the small purchase threshold. Purchase documentation must show
that good value was received for the money spent. For example, documentation would
include price comparisons to amounts previously paid for similar goods or services or
comments from peer organizations as to what was paid in similar situations.
Where one or more of the above circumstances occur, the procurement committee may
decide to waive the requirement or the competitive bidding process with discussed the
chief executive of the organization. Such a decision shall be fully documented and
justified in writing and kept in the procurement file for later inspection.

printing supplies, stationery, computers and software, food & beverage, hall room,
accommodation, vehicle rent as per kilometer basis and pharmaceutical supplies.

Framework arrangements can represent a ‘smarter’ way of purchasing. Most
organizations have limited resources, so using a procurement framework can save
significant time and money whilst servicing a local requirement and ensuring
accountable decision-making when choosing the work.
