1.1 Background:

Considering human as the Supreme Being and more valuable than all the wealth of the world
MUKTI COX’S BAZAR has started its journey at Cox’s Bazar district on 1996 as a non-
government development organization to materialize the dream a peaceful universal society. From
the very beginning of the organization, it has been implementing various programs especially for
the poor and vulnerable women making them aware of various livelihood issues through family
approach at urban and rural areas of Cox’s Bazar district.

To materialize this dream, its effort is going on since 1996. With the help of all concerned its name
and fame is spreading all over the country. We have firm belief that in near future Mukti will become
familiar to the international arena. The workers and all concerned adhere to the core belief that
Mukti is the organization of those for whom we are working, a bit advance of the poor, helpless,
oppressed, and unprivileged women. Steps were and are being taken to save them from the clutches
of poverty and social instrument of tyranny. But if we are let to speak the truth, we are not ready to
call these measures adequate or very effective to bring overnight changes in a society where
problems are accumulated for ages. Help with money should not be a system or procedure of uplift.
Money may be given to promote business, to create job and income generating activities. But to
keep these necessary creation and prevailing machineries of income sustainable and functioning
ethical, logistic and technical supports are being given which we think will bring socio-economic
changes in the target society. Alongside we are trying to link with the administration and
peacekeeping agencies to guarantee and preserve their basic rights. Through different projects
designed for the purpose we have taken substantial measures to make them aware of their short
comings, impediments and problems they are facing, the prejudices and superstitions they are
becoming target of, and to find out way to get rid of all these. We have ambition to make our target
society a poverty-free welfare society where men, women and children can live in peace and
harmony, with dignity and honor, with liberty and freedom.

The manual is to be considered as the instrument of sound financial management as well as effective
accounting administration and is intended to provide written guidelines to all concerned with a view
to facilitating proper control over financial activities of the organization. It will be of great assistance
in bringing overall financial discipline and uniformity as well as cohesion amongst the different
areas of the financial management. The basic requirement for ensuring a satisfactory and meaningful
financial management is to establish a comprehensive system with well-defined procedure so that
the accounting records are maintained accurately, and the financial statements can be prepared to
give correct information as and when desired.

CHAPTER - 3 ACCOUNTING

3.1 Necessity:

For financial support for its activities Mukti Cox’s Bazar may depend on donation from local &
foreign donors, Government grants, and also on its own contribution. That is why systematic
recording of financial transactions and timely reporting to stakeholders through preparation of
proper financial statements in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles are
essential. The main objective of establishing the manual is to provide a systematic & acceptable
basis of maintaining accounts as well as supporting documents.

3.2 Basis of Accounting:

Accounts of the Mukti Cox’ Bazar need to be maintained on generally accepted accounting
principles (GAAP) and IFRS under the historical cost convention. The Mukti Cox’s Bazar shall
maintain its accounts on accrual basis but as per requirement of NGOAB and donor respective
accounts will be maintain on cash basis. The financial statements will be prepared on both cash and
accrual basis as per required and records will be maintained in double entry system. All incomes
received during a particular year shall be accounted for in the same year. Any part of income, which
is committed for subsequent year(s) shall not be treated as income in the year of receipt. All
expenditures incurred, paid and accrued shall be considered as expenditure in the year to which it
relates. The organization may be required to follow the guidelines/ instructions of the donors as
stipulated in the agreement and in that case the agreed term will prevail even if it contravenes the
provisions of this manual

3.3 Period of Accounting:

Annual accounts of Mukti Cox’s Bazar are maintained following the Fiscal year i.e., July to June.
However, the organization may be required to follow instructions of the donors regarding closing
of accounts of project funded by them.

3.4 Recording of Transaction:

Money receipt: The organization receives fund from the donors as grant through cheque/draft
subject to the terms of the agreement between the donor and the organization. Other sources of
income are local contributions, member’s subscription donation, income from training, sale of
health card, BCC materials, service charge, old/obsolete assets and waste papers etc.

Money receipts in format given as Annex-1 shall be prepared and issued to the concerned parties /
payees on receipt of any fund/ income by the organization Money receipt forms must be printed and
pre-numbered. Money receipt will be written by carbon process in triplicate. Original copy of receipt
will be given to the payee and the second copy will be attached with the credit voucher. The third
copy will be tied serially in a book and preserved.

All the receipts in cash or through cheque shall be deposited, at the earliest opportunity, to the bank
filling up the bank's pay-in-Slip form and should be recorded in the cheque Receipt Register, then
it will be entered in the Accounts Software or Automation Software to preparing credit voucher in
support by the Finance official.

Cash/ cheque payment: All payments made in cash or through cheque/bank transfer advice shall
be processed through the Debit voucher (payment voucher) and entered in the Accounts Software
or Automation Software for ultimate debit to the appropriate head of accounts. All Debit vouchers
must be supported by the documentary evidence such as requisition/indent, quotation, comparative

4.1 Introduction:

In general, the accounts in the following forms and books of record should be prepared and
maintained by Mukti Cox’s Bazar. These accounting forms and books should be used as documents
for various analysis and interpretation of daily financial transactions of Mukti Cox’s Bazar in terms
of actual (cash) and accrual system. Characteristics and utilities of different forms and books of
record are described here under in details for recording financial transactions systematically.

4.2 Voucher:

Voucher is the primary source of information in the accounting process. Vouchers are prepared on
the basis of invoice or memorandum of services as evidence of a transaction. Before a transaction
is completed, it has to be recorded in voucher. Usually, vouchers are of following four types:

Debit Voucher/Payment Voucher: Debit vouchers are prepared for recording all revenue and
capital expenditure such as staff salary & benefit, office rent, plant & machinery, furniture &
fixtures, repairs & maintenance, stationery etc. Necessary original supporting documents should be
uploaded with debit voucher except contract documents to be filed separately. This voucher has
other alternative names, such as, payment voucher, disbursement voucher etc. Format for debit
voucher is given in Annex-3 for manual recording and for Accounts Software or Automation
Software generated by its self.

Credit Voucher/ Receipt Voucher: Credit vouchers are prepared as the basis for recording all
receipts including income, such as, amount receive from donor, advance refunded by staff, amount
receive by any other means through sale, transfer etc. Before approving this voucher, it is to be
ensured that all necessary supporting documents have been attached. Format for credit voucher is
given in Annex-4 for manual recording and for accounting software generated by its self.

Transfer Voucher/Contra Voucher: Cash withdrawn from and deposited to bank are recorded in
books of accounts on the basis of transfer voucher. Generally, there is no need of supporting
documents for transfer voucher. Passbook, bank statements, deposit slip and counter foil of cheque
are considered to be supporting documents for transfer voucher. So, these should be kept properly
in a file at the office. Format for transfer voucher is given in Annex-5 for manual recording and for
accounting ERP based Accounts Software Automation software generated by its self.

Journal Voucher: Deduction from salary, depreciation on fixed assets, adjustment of advance,
income tax deduction, provision for expenses etc. are recorded in the books of accounts on the basis
of journal vouchers. Required supporting documents are to be attached with such voucher before
obtaining approved. Format for journal voucher is given in Annex-6 for manual recording and for
accounting ERP based Accounts Software Automation software generated by its self.

4.3 Accounting Software or Automation Software:

Accounts Software is the most important and primary book of accounts which bears the record of
all cash/cheque transactions. Finance Department will maintain double column cash/Bank book in
which debit voucher will be recorded in the payment column and credit voucher will be recorded in
the receipt column chronologically.

4.4 Conclusion:

The maintenance of records listed above is unavoidable for ensuring the existence of proper internal
control. Proper care must be taken for their preservation. Special attention should also be paid to be
sure that the entries in these records are made as carefully as possible. Overwriting, erasing use of
correcting fluid and tearing off "t of page for wrong entries should invariably be avoided. If an entry
is found to be erroneous, after inserting correct data and deleting the wrong one an initial should be

given.

6.1 Introduction:

Financial reports and statements are the ultimate products of the finance and accounts department.
All financial transactions occurred or generated throughout the year are recorded in a set of books
and records. A systematic recording system facilitates an accountant to prepare financial reports
accurately at any point of time.

Financial statements and reports contain a consolidated typical presentation of financial data.
Statutory financial statements are prepared in accordance with International Accounting standards
(IAS) and other applicable rules and regulations. In other cases, financial reports and statements are
prepared so as to meet the requirements of the users.

6.2 Internal Reports:

Mukti Cox’s Bazar usually monitor financial transaction daily basis and could generate report from
its Accounts Software or automation software centrally as and when required, however, Mukti
Cox’s Bazar Head Office Finance Department receive and review report as monthly basis from
Micro Credit Assistant Program (MCAP) and the project quarterly basis, yearly consolidate report
from the MCAP & Projects.

Monthly: At the end of each month the following reports/statements are prepared by MCAP usually
within 05 working days of the following month.

a) Trail Balance

b) Statement of Receipts & Payment

c) Statement of Income & Expenditure

d) Statement of Financial Position/Balance Sheet
e) Budget Variance Statement.

f) Bank Reconciliation Statement

Apart from this, reports as required by PKSF & MRA will be regularly prepared by the Micro Credit
Assistant Program

Quarterly: At the end of each quarter the following reports/statements are usually prepared for each
project within 05 working days of following month of the quarter.

a) Trail Balance

b) Statement of Receipts & Payment

c) Statement of Income & Expenditure

d) Statement of Financial Position/Balance Sheet

e) Budget Variance Statement.

12.1 Accounting Responsibility:
The responsibility of preparation of the financial statement’s rests with the management. In his/her

capacity as the Chief Executive of the Organization, the Chief Executive will be responsible for the
overall accuracy and authenticity of the financial statements, and the accounting and other books
and records on the basis of which such statements are prepared.

The Chief of Finance & Accounts has overall responsibility for maintaining the accounting system
and books of records. He/she is required to discharge this responsibility through a proper and
efficient system ensuring effective internal controls, segregation of duties, and supervisory controls.

Every person whose duty is to prepare the primary documents such as bills, vouchers etc. is
personally responsible for their completeness and accuracy.

An officer who signs or countersigns any primary document accounting returns or certificate is
personally responsible for the facts stated therein so far as it is his/her duty to know or to the extent
to which he/she may reasonably be expected to be aware of them.

12.2 The Finance and Accounts Department:
The Finance and Accounts (F&A) Department is headed by Coordinator of Finance & Accounts

and is directly responsible to the Chief Executive. The department is responsible for keeping proper
records of all financial transactions and prepare various management and financial reports, as well
as give necessary advice and assistance in the efficient and effective management of the
organization. This department will also monitor the accounts of partner organization and help them
to keep these records as per the organization’s requirements.

12.3 Introduction of Internal Control:

The internal ‘control system is unavoidable for an organization for efficient management of its
financial matter as well as for maintaining the continuity and accuracy of data pertaining to in the
arena of its financial affairs. With a view to ensuring an effective and efficient workflow at all stages
of transaction procedure, it is essential to segregate a specific job in different segments and make a
meaningful division of work to accomplish that job.

12.4 Internal Control -& Management Function:
The term controlling is used in management to describe the function of measuring and correcting

activities to make sure that plans and objectives are met. Internal control is regarded as the
management function of acting to ensure that objectives are achieved. It starts with the definition of
aims and objectives and continues through an organization's plans, structure and activities. It can be
identified in policies, procedures, regulations, directions, manuals and other arrangements. It
encompasses the procedures for directing, supervising, monitoring and reporting on all operations,
functions and activities.

12.11 The Purchase and Payment Control Cycle:

The initial running in the purchase control cycle is the raising of a purchase requisition in accordance
with the approved procurement policy. Upon approval, quotations are collected; the selected
supplier is notified through issue of a purchase order. The goods are received by the project or
logistic personnel, physically checked for condition and quantity and ensure conformity with the
purchase order. The purchase order and suppliers’ invoice are matched as the initial step of the
payment cycle. In the next step, the Accounts Department will verify all necessary documents as
per needs and pay the price to the concerned vendor or supplier or service provider through account
payee check or bank transfer and confirm the acknowledgement. Payment must be made within ten
working days of receipt of the invoice with proper documentation by the Accounts Department.

In the process for payment of the invoice the following must have been carried out prior to writing
the cheque:

1. That goods received physically matched to purchase order and invoice;
2. That invoiced unit price is checked to agree with the price per purchase order; and
3. That arithmetical checks carried out on invoiced total cost.

12.12 Internal Control:

This is a crucially important control activity (technique) that involves assigning job duties so that
no single employee can both perpetrate a misappropriation and conceal the misappropriation from
timely detection. one major segregation of duties concept is that no employee should have more
than one of the following types of job duties: maintaining custody over (access to) an asset,
maintaining accounting records for that asset, and authorizing use of that asset.

12.13 Level of Control:

The nature, extent and strength of controls in different organizations may vary. The controls used
will depend on the nature or operations, the importance of the system, the degree of risk, personnel
and many other factors.

12.14 Cost of Controls:

Management should consider the cost of controls in relation to the risk and exposure they cover.
Control usually costs money and the wrong balance of controls may make an organization
inefficient and lethargic. The need for and costs of individual controls should be balanced against
the wider needs for control over economy, efficiency and effectiveness. The choice of control
methods should be based on a comparison of costs and expected benefits or reduction of risk.

12.15 The Limitations of Internal Controls:

Internal control cannot by itself guarantee the achievement of objectives, existence of efficient and
effective administration and the completeness and accuracy in processing data.

Internal control cannot be a proof against fraud, especially by those holding positions of authority
and trust and through collusion of concerned employees’ internal control that depends on separation
of functions can be weekend by undesirable collusion. The competence and integrity or management
and staff operating controls can be fostered by selection and training but cannot be guaranteed.

Internal control system in an organization develops over time. It is strengthened by management
initiative and its response regularity activity (e.g., internal audit). It may be weakened by accident
or faulty design by management and by failure to respond to changing circumstances.

R — 13 FIXED ASSETS MANAG

13.1 Introduction:

Fixed assets have the fundamental characteristic that they are held to be used with the object of
further use and are not primarily intended for disposal in the ordinary course of business. Fixed
Assets are held by an organization for use in the operation or production or supply of goods and
services or for rental to others or for administrative purposes.

Land including its development generally has an indefinite life and is, therefore, not a depreciable
asset. Buildings, however, do have a definite life and are treated as depreciable assets. Thus, for
accounting purposes the value of land including its development and buildings should be segregated.

Safeguard of fixed assets is the management's responsibility. Control over fixed assets is an essential
part of routine management exercise. Mukti Cox’s Bazar management/ employees shall ensure the
best utilization and accounting for all of its fixed assets.

13.5 Depreciation:

Every asset has a useful life-span which varies depending on the particular characteristics of the
asset. Under the universally accepted simple straight-line method, the purchase value of an asset
when divided by its expected useful life. (In years) results in that asset's yearly depreciation.
Depreciation for the accounting period is charged to statement of income and expenditure.

The above depreciation method adopted should be applied consistently from year to year unless
special circumstances justify a change.

Annual depreciation rates for different types of assets of Mukti Cox's Bazar will be determined as
per NBR guidelines.

Asset addition within first six months of the year will be charged depreciation for full year and those
within last six months will be charged for half year.

Assets disposed of during the accounting year need not be charged any depreciation for that year.

ID number to secure existence. However, only one Taka value shall be kept against each of such

assets.

13.9 Accounting for Disposal of Fixed Assets:
At the time of disposal of the asset, the value of accumulated depreciation as well as the cost of the
assets shall be taken out from the Fixed Asset Register.

In case of sale, if the sales price of the disposed asset is greater than the written Down value (WDV),
then the difference between the sales price and WDV shall be treated as 'Profit on Disposal of Fixed
Assets,

On the other hand, if the sales price of the disposed asset is less than the WDV, then the difference
between the sales price and WDV shall be treated as 'Loss on Disposal of Fixed Assets’.

16.1 Introduction:

Most of the projects of Mukti Cox’s Bazar are financed by donors. So, ensure highest level of
transference, accuracy and effective use of donor grants an accounting treatment for donor grant is
one of the major issues in Mukti Cox’s Bazar 's introduce (Online Automation accounting software
Accounts Software) accounting system. Unlike other items, it has got some special characteristics.
International Accounting Standard-20 (IAS-20) spells out the systems for Accounting for
Government Grant (Government refers to government, government agencies and similar bodies
whether local, national or international). Mukti Cox’s Bazar's donors are, therefore, covered by the
said standard. Keeping in view this aspect, the related accounting systems have been devised in this
policy.
As per IAS-20 (Accounting for Government Grants and Disclosure of Government Assistance),
Mukti Cox’s Bazar's grant related accounting issues would be identified under the following
categories:

>» Donor fund-General

> Donor fund-investment in fixed assets

Grant related to assets and revenue will be accounted for as per following norms:

16.2 Grant Related to Assets:

Grant related to assets including non-monetary grants at fair value should be presented in the
Balance Sheet by setting the grant as Deferred Income.

16.3 Grant Related to Income/Revenue:

Grant should be recognized as income over the period’s equivalent to with the related cost of the
project/program, which they are intended to compensate on a systematic basis. Grant should not be
credited directly to own fund. The project overhead cost (as per donor agreed) will be treated as
income and it will be used any kind of development of the organization.

16.4 Donor Fund-General:

When donation is received, it is to be recorded under this account irrespective of the nature of
donation. Thereafter, donations utilized for assets are transferred to respective accounts. Donation
utilized for revenue type expenses for a period is transferred to income. The balance remaining in
this account represents unutilized donor fund.

Sometimes Mukti Cox’s Bazar faces shortage of fund in donation account. In such a situation, it
creates receivable on donor's account and this accrued income is matched against expenditure or
Mukti Cox’s Bazar arranges (through short term loan) the deficit financing from its own fund. On
creation of receivable, donation receivable account is debited and donor fund general account is
credited.

16.5 Donor Fund-Investment in Fixed Assets:

When donation is utilized for fixed assets, related donation is transferred from, Donor Fund-General
and recorded under this head and transferred portion of the fund is treated as Deferred Income. At
the end of the year, income will be recognized from this fund to the extent of depreciation charge
on the related asset.

16.6 Completion of a Phase/Project:

On completion of a phase of a project the unspent donor funds are treated unutilized fund as
liabilities.

Budget is a plan of operation expressed in monetary terms. Mukti Cox’s Bazar project budget

Provides an estimate of funds required for achieving goals and objectives of the project. It is also a

+ Expression of broad policy guidelines in terms of specific activities;

* Provides guidelines for taking administrative decision;

+ Sets limits of expenditures in specific area of activities as well as delineates the total
provision;

Provides means of monitoring of performance and evaluation of impact of a project.

17.5 Budgetary Control:

The importance of having an approved budget and exercising affective budgetary control at all level
of Mukti Cox’s Bazar is highly emphasized. In all cases expenditure must remain within approved
budget limit till the concerned authorities revise it considering the necessity.

In case of bilateral projects, budget limits are determined according to the project agreement. Such
agreement also specifies the limit of flexibility in budget usage either in terms of percentage or
amount. In case of any unusual requirement, budget overrun up to a maximum of 10% under a
particular sub-line item is allowable. Any expenditure in excess of 10% will require prior approval
from Donor/ Chief Executive as the case may be. However, in no case, the actual expenditure shall
exceed the budget limit of control/main head of budget. Similar permission will also be required
from the Grantor/Donor for any emergency expenditure outside the head of expenses in the
approved budget and for expenditure exceeding the total approved budget.

For proper monitoring of the budget utilization, the Coordinator Finance & Accounts will provide
a set of financial statements showing comparison of actual expenditure against budget with
percentage of variance to the Chief Executive quarterly. The Chief Executive will report on the
financial performance of Mukti to the Executive Committee.

17.6 Budget Revision:

In case there is of shortage of fund or unavoidable requirement of additional expenditure beyond
allowable limit becomes unavoidable, the management may revise the approved budget at any time.
In such cases, the revised budget has to be placed before the Executive Committee /donor for
approval.

17.7 Budget Submission to NGO Affairs Bureau:

When the budgets are approved both internally by the Organization and externally by the potential
foreign donors, the organization will present the budget to the NGO Affairs Bureau in prescribed
Form of NGOAB for their approval. All departments of the organization namely Administration,
Finance & Accounts, and Program shall work collaboratively to prepare the prescribed forms with
all required information and Annexes. The forms properly reviewed by the Coordinator Finance and
Accounts and approved by the Chief Executive, will be submitted to the NGO Affairs Bureau for
their approval.

17.8 Line-item flexibility of budget:

A budget under each line item is usually fixed, and cannot be exceeded without prior approval of
the donor. In some cases, minor line-item flexibility (maximum 10%) of actual expenditure is
allowed in individual line items, provided the total budget is not exceeded.

Transfer of budget allocation from one line item to another is not allowed, except with the prior
permission of the donor and the NGO Affairs Bureau. For this purpose, a revised budget has to be
submitted and approved both by the donors and the NGO Affairs Bureau (if necessary).

17.9 Budget analysis and monitoring:

The Coordinator of Program/Head of Program/Focal of Program & Coordinator (Finance &
Accounts) is responsible for making financial transactions and monitoring expenses with due
emphasis of monthly budget balance distributed by the Finance Department. The Coordinator of
Program/Head of Program/Focal of Program & Coordinator (Finance & Accounts) is to take
corrective measures whenever negative variance in individual code-wise budget targets is expected
to occur and give satisfactory explanation to major deviations of physical/financial budget targets.

It is desirable that for all practical purposes budget are to be analyzed monthly upon receipt of
monthly budget balance and the result of analysis are to be discussed with the Coordinator (Finance
& Accounts) for smooth budgetary control. However, the Finance Department is to analyze the
Annual Budget situation on quarterly basis based on the printed form.

1. Fundamental principles

The audit will be carried out by an independent auditor (one who does not have privileged

standards (ISA-Intcrnational Standards on Auditing, and IFAC INTERNATIONAL

the country in which the mandate is carricd out.

2. Objectives of the audit

The objectives of the auditare:

2.1 Examination of the financial regularity, through the control and evaluation of the
exactness, exhaustiveness and fairness of the various accounting transactions, either in
full or by sampling, according to need and risk evaluation (before and during the course
of the audit);

2.2 Examination of the conformity with Organization objectives through the control and
evaluation of transactions, expenditures and receipts compared with the basic documents (Core
Program -Micro Credit as well as its different, project including, programs of activitics, contracts,
budgets etc.)

2.3 Examination of the economical and efficient conduct of business through the
control and evaluation of
a) minimization of the cost of the resources utilized for the activities, always
ensuring’an appropriate quality
b) comparison’ of expenditures with corresponding budget positions and the
control and evaluation of any explanations for divergences:

2.4 Examination of the appropriateness, the relevance and the functioning of the Internal
Control System, through the control and evaluation of the
a) Internal organization (structures, functions, tasks, authority, responsibilities,
methods, procedures, etc.),
b) existence, respect and application of laws, regulations and instructions
¢) protection of resources and assets
d) prevention of errors and fraud
¢) quality and viability of the information system and the reporting

2.5 Examination of the observance of the Micro Credit operation policy and procedure
align with PKSF and Bangladesh Bank Micro Credit regulator rules and
applicable law and procedures.
